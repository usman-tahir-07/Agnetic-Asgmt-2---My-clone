# My Advice for Junior Students, and My Goals

## Advice I'd give a first-year CS student

Ok so, if you're just starting computer science, here's what I'd tell you:

- **Don't just memorise definitions or copy code.** It might get you through one quiz, but it won't help you later when the questions change.
- **Understand the basics properly.** Variables, loops, functions, tables, keys. Everything later is built on these.
- **Practise regularly.** Programming and SQL need way more practice than you think. A little every day beats a lot the night before.
- **Build small projects as you learn.** Even a tiny project teaches you how things connect. My DBMS project CaseTrack taught me more than reading about databases ever did.
- **Read your error messages.** When code gives an error, read it and try to understand what caused it before you start changing random things.
- **Ask questions early.** If you don't get something, ask your teacher, a classmate or even an AI tool, but ask before the confusion piles up.
- **Revise before quizzes.** Short revision notes plus a few MCQs work really well.
- **Don't compare your progress with everyone else's.** Everyone learns at a different speed. Focus on improving compared to yourself last month.
- **Use AI wisely.** It's great for explanations and getting unstuck, but make sure you understand what it gives you.

## My goals

Short term, my goal is to get better at programming, databases and full-stack development, make my projects stronger, and become more confident at solving problems independently.

Longer term, I'd like to grow into a software developer who builds real-world products, most likely on the backend or full-stack side, since connecting web apps to databases is the work I've enjoyed most so far.

I haven't fixed a plan yet for after graduation, whether that's a job, further studies or something abroad. My priority right now is building strong skills; I'll decide the rest when the time comes.
