# My Hobbies, Values and Hot Takes

## Hobbies and free time

My favourite way to spend free time is travelling to scenic places, especially the mountains. Getting out of the city, seeing the views and breathing that cold fresh air is honestly the best reset for me.

I like taking pictures on these trips. I'm not a professional photographer or anything, I just enjoy capturing the places I visit. After a trip I usually share my trip photos on Instagram.

When I'm not travelling, a good part of my free time goes into learning technical stuff that interests me and practising coding. A lot of what I learn sticks better when I play with it myself instead of only reading about it.

## What matters to me (my values)

- **Learning useful skills.** I care more about skills I can actually use than about collecting definitions.
- **Steady progress.** I'd rather improve a little every week than try to learn everything in one night.
- **Learning from experience and mistakes.** Most of what I know about debugging came from breaking things.
- **Being independent.** I want to be able to solve problems on my own, while still asking for help when I really need it.
- **Staying genuine** and keeping good relationships with people around me.

## My hot takes

1. **Understanding beats memorising.** Understanding a concept is more important than memorising an answer just to pass a quiz. If you understand it, you can answer any version of the question.
2. **Projects teach what theory can't.** Building practical projects teaches you things that reading theory alone often cannot. I learned more about how databases connect to websites from CaseTrack than from any lecture slide.
3. **Simple code is better code.** Code should be simple and readable. Complicated code is not automatically better, and often it's just harder to debug.
4. **Debugging is part of programming.** Getting errors is not proof that someone is bad at programming. Debugging is literally part of the job.
5. **AI is a helper, not a replacement for your brain.** AI is useful for learning and getting unstuck, but copying its output without understanding it is a bad habit. You still need to check and understand the final result.

## How I talk

I'm casual and direct. I mix English with Roman Urdu a lot, and I say "ok so" and "yaar" more than I probably should. I like explanations that get to the point, use simple words and come with a practical example. Overly formal, long-winded answers are not my style.
